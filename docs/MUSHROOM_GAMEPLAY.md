# Mushroom gameplay across Java and Bedrock

Use these outcomes when reviewing a gameplay change, investigating a family playtest report, or adding a regression. The player actions and expected results are shared. Java Fabric GameTests and the live-client bridge drive Java, and Bedrock GameTest simulated players drive Bedrock. The [compatibility matrix](COMPATIBILITY.md#gameplay-checks-by-edition) shows which checks already exist.

## Behaviors to check

| Scenario | Set up and act | Expected result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adopt one Mushroom | Two players each adopt a dog in the same world using the edition's normal interaction. Each tries to adopt again. | Each player owns exactly one dog. A rejected adoption preserves the original dog and any unspent item. |
| Sit and follow | Use the usual empty-hand interaction. Walk away, come back, and ask him to follow again. Have another player try to change the order. | The owner can switch between sit and follow. A guest cannot change ownership or the sit order. Check daycare and Creative separately. |
| Share a walk | The owner fits a harness. An invited guest attaches a lead, walks Mushroom, feeds him while he is leashed, and releases the lead. | The lead stays attached through feeding. The guest can walk and release him. Owner, harness, and dog identity survive the handoff. |
| Stay at daycare | Leave him seated beyond 32 blocks on foot. Try a closed door, an open door with a clear route, and a lead tied to a fence. | A closed door or tied lead keeps him in place. In Bedrock, an awake, unleashed Mushroom can walk out through the open door and accepts the sit command again afterward. |
| Feed and water | Feed treats and ordinary player food at full and partial health in Creative and Survival. Try ordinary food as a guest and as the owner holding the lead. Use the food and water bowls twice in one game day and again the next day. | Use the [shared treat expectations](../scripts/fixtures/mushroom-treat-cases.json) and [ordinary food expectations](../scripts/fixtures/mushroom-food-cases.json) for health and item counts. Ordinary food from the owner keeps the lead attached. Each filled bowl is used once per day and becomes empty. The next day's refill works. |
| Sleep and wake | Give him a clear, dry dog bed or doghouse. Play through dusk and dawn. Walk or fly past him while he sleeps. Repeat with the bed blocked or wet. | He settles on the supported bed, rests with his head still, and wakes at dawn. A blocked or wet bed never becomes a sleep spot. Check the current Creative takeoff difference below. |
| Follow by air | With Mushroom following and awake, take off near him in Creative and fly on a clear route. Land and continue on foot. Repeat while he is seated or leashed. | A free dog follows nearby flight and lands safely. A seated or leashed dog stays put. Exercise distance and catch-up separately from nearby flight. |
| Fetch | Throw a ball, chew toy, or bone. Drop a ball amid short grass and then two-block tall grass. Let him finish, then repeat and interrupt after pickup by asking him to sit or rejoining. | Grass fetch returns exactly one original ball without clearing the grass. A real toy survives interruption and can be recovered exactly once. A Creative practice throw leaves the original in inventory and never creates an extra collectible. |
| Reopen and update | Save while two players have adopted dogs. Reopen the same disposable world, then upgrade it from the previous shipped build. | Ownership, sit orders, harnesses, inventory, and existing dogs survive. Each player can still command their own Mushroom. |

Use regular player interactions for the action under test. Commands can set up terrain, time, and camera views. Check application state after the action, such as the dog ID, owner, position, pose, and item counts. A successful button press alone does not establish the outcome.

## Java-to-Bedrock parity audit

This inventory follows Java's player-facing behaviors and the active Bedrock pack. Each open scenario is a concrete starting point for a native test. The server checks and the rendered-client checklist cover different observations of the same gameplay.

| Area | Existing coverage | Missing parity or test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adoption and identity | Java GameTests cover taming and independent ownership. Bedrock covers egg use, sleep adoption, and a loaded dog's bedtime recovery. | Across logout, death, portal travel, and distance beyond loaded chunks, prove the same identity returns or a replacement becomes eligible. Cover existing saves and simultaneous egg use. [Core](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/128) · [Recovery](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/131) |
| Sit, follow, and walks | Both exercise lead borrowing. Bedrock checks the open and closed daycare door and a tied lead. | Java keeps the manual sit order. Should this edition also follow on foot through an open doorway after 32 blocks? Add both control cases before changing the order policy. Verify native leash attachment and touch prompts on a client. [Daycare](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/156) |
| Everyday care | Shared Java and Bedrock fixtures check treats, ordinary food, and health and inventory. Native bowl checks cover use and daily limits. | Bedrock has daily bowls but no Java hunger, thirst, energy, mood or starvation system. Replay eating after a day without bowls, offline owner safety, and emergency feeding during sleep. A healthy dog should accept an owner's meal while its bowl limit stays intact. [Care](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/129) |
| Outdoor requests, moods, and alerts | Java has outdoor relief, peaceful-animal barking, hostile hesitation and trust behavior. | Rest | Both have supported beds and doghouse rest checks. Bedrock preserves sleep during Creative flight. | Java currently wakes the dog on Creative takeoff. Record stillness and mattress alignment on an actual client. [Care](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/129) · [Rendering](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/148) |
| Retrieve and return | Both exercise ground fetch, grass, shallow water, interruption, and cargo persistence. Bedrock checks covered water and unsafe ledges. | Bedrock bones currently use Drop Item. Compare Java's Use input and Bedrock's Drop Item on a controller. Fill inventory in both modes, disconnect during a carry, and kill the carrier in a disposable fixture to prove one real toy survives and a practice copy never becomes collectible. Test deeper shoreline recovery separately. [Fetch](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/130) |
| Flight, catch-up, and tricks | Both cover following a nearby Creative owner in flight. Java has collision-checked recovery, airborne fetch, and flight tricks. | Bedrock's airborne follow stops past 48 blocks and has no equivalent airborne toy recovery or loop-the-loop and barrel roll. Replay just inside and outside the cutoff, takeoff after sleep, a grounded owner behind terrain, a cliff fetch through landing or rejoin, and a seated or tied dog. | Vehicles, water, and dimensions | Java GameTests cover boarding, occupied vehicles, owner-assisted exits, submerged recovery, and follow versus sit behavior on portal travel. | Bedrock needs the three separate paths. [Travel](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/131) |
| Squirrels and exploration | Java has squirrel spawns, chase and canopy tests, plus tests for structure scent policy. | Bedrock has no squirrel, tree route or structure scent equivalent. Prove the underside-to-canopy route and return first, then chase interruption. Any scent implementation must be able to find and lead to a real structure within a bounded scan. [Squirrels](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/132) · [Scent](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/133) |
| Controller, touch, and appearance | Both have server-side coverage. Render previews inspect Bedrock assets in isolation. | Use shipped client builds to check the interaction prompt, eye and nose visibility, ears, sitting legs, lead endpoint, sleep gaze and flight animations. Repeat on a spare Realm slot to verify the update path. [Client gate](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/148) |

## Running the checks

1. Start with the regression closest to the change. Add a shared fixture when both editions can assert the same values, using the treat cases as the pattern. Where a scenario depends on real navigation, run the same setup and outcome on each native runner.
2. Put the short cases in CI and run mixed play sessions for transitions between them. Record the exact build and game version, seed, actions, elapsed time, and failing observation. Replay a family report on the affected build before using a new build to prove the fix.
3. Check appearance and ordinary input on a real client. For a face change, keep the same camera and lighting and compare front and oblique views. Look for ears that lean away from the center of the head, visible eyes, and a solid nose. In gameplay, check the seated legs, lead attachment at the harness, and still head during sleep. Record Switch, mobile, and Realm results by device and build.

Use the [cross-edition test plan](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/147) and [rendered-client checks](https://github.com/alicon/minecraft-mods/issues/148) to record new evidence and coverage gaps.
